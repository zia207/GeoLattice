Water Cloud Model and Surface Scattering Inversion

Change detection ignores the canopy; physical retrievals model it. The Water Cloud Model (WCM) treats a crop as a cloud of water droplets above the soil: the canopy scatters some of the radar signal back and attenuates the rest on its way to and from the ground. Calibrated against field measurements, it can be inverted for soil moisture under the crop. This notebook uses the SMAPVEX16 Iowa campaign, in which NASA and university teams measured surface soil moisture on 32 corn and soybean fields in early June and August 2016, matched to Sentinel-1 backscatter, its incidence angle and Sentinel-2 NDVI. It calibrates the WCM per crop, tests it with leave-one-field-out cross-validation, compares vegetation descriptors, tries bare-soil scattering models (Oh, IEM) on the early-season fields, and maps soil moisture over the campaign area.

Overview

The Water Cloud Model

For a vegetation descriptor V (NDVI, LAI or vegetation water content) and incidence angle t (Attema and Ulaby 1978):

sigma0 = A V^E cos(t) (1 - tau2) + tau2 sigma0_soil, with tau2 = exp(-2 B V / cos(t))

The first term is the direct backscatter of the canopy, the second the soil backscatter attenuated twice (two-way) by the canopy. The soil term is commonly linear in decibels, sigma0_soil(dB) = C + D theta. A, B, C and D are calibrated per crop against field data; E is often fixed at 1.

Surface scattering models

Model Type Inputs Validity
Oh (1992, 2004) semi-empirical, from truck-mounted radar theta (or permittivity), rms height s, angle ks 0.1 to 6, theta 0.04 to 0.29
Dubois et al. (1995) semi-empirical permittivity, s, angle ks < 2.5, angle > 30 degrees, NDVI < 0.4
IEM (Fung et al. 1992) physical, integral equation permittivity, s, correlation length l, correlation function ks < 3

They need roughness, which is rarely known; inversion therefore searches soil moisture and roughness together in a look-up table (LUT), and works only on bare or nearly bare fields.

Vegetation descriptors

NDVI from optical images is easy to obtain but saturates in dense canopies and needs a clear day; radar itself offers descriptors such as the cross-polarization ratio VH/VV, which grows with volume scattering and is available on every pass.

Physical Retrieval with GeoLattice

Function Purpose
sm.water_cloud_model forward WCM in dB
sm.calibrate_wcm least squares fit of A, B, C, D per group
sm.invert_wcm analytical inversion to soil moisture, per group
sm.oh_model, sm.dubois_model bare-soil backscatter
sm.iem_lut, sm.invert_lut IEM look-up table and its inversion
sd.smapvex16_samples, sd.smapvex16_scene campaign samples and a scene on a 20 m grid

Learning objectives

  1. Match field soil moisture to SAR backscatter, incidence angle and an optical vegetation index.
  2. Calibrate the Water Cloud Model per crop and judge it by leave-one-field-out cross-validation.
  3. Compare NDVI and the radar cross-polarization ratio as vegetation descriptors.
  4. Run Oh and IEM bare-soil models and invert a look-up table on early-season fields.
  5. Map soil moisture under corn and soybean.

Data

Dataset Content
SMAPVEX16 Iowa ThetaProbe (NSIDC SV16I_TPSM) 0 to 5 cm soil moisture at 16 points per field, 32 fields, 12 days in May to June and August 2016
Sentinel-1 RTC and GRD annotations VV and VH gamma0 at 20 m on three relative orbits (63, 143, 165) and the incidence angle of each field
Sentinel-2 L2A NDVI at each field, cloud-masked and interpolated to the radar dates
USDA CDL 2016 corn and soybean fields for the map

Calibrating the Water Cloud Model

The model is fitted per crop on all samples, with NDVI as the vegetation descriptor and E = 1, once without constraints and once with the soil sensitivity D bounded to 20 to 40 dB per m3 m-3, the range reported for C-band over agricultural soils.

Code 
free = sm.calibrate_wcm(df.vv_db, df.sm, df.ndvi, df.incidence, groups=df.crop)  # unconstrained per-crop fit
BOUNDS = {"D": (20.0, 40.0)}  # physically plausible soil sensitivity (dB per m3/m3)
cal = sm.calibrate_wcm(df.vv_db, df.sm, df.ndvi, df.incidence, groups=df.crop, bounds=BOUNDS)  # constrained fit

Without constraints the corn fit finds almost no soil sensitivity (D of about 1.4 dB per m3 m-3), because the corn samples barely show one; inverting such a model amplifies every 0.1 dB of noise into large soil moisture errors. Bounding D to the physical range moves both crops to the lower bound of 20 dB per m3 m-3 at a small cost in calibration RMSE (1.4 dB).

Leave-one-field-out validation

The calibration RMSE flatters the model, because every field helped fit the parameters it is tested on. Leaving out each field in turn, calibrating on the others and inverting the left-out field gives an honest estimate of the retrieval error at a new field. The baseline to beat is simply predicting the mean soil moisture of the campaign.

Code 
def loo(desc, bounds=None):  # leave-one-field-out retrieval with a given vegetation descriptor
    out = []  # predictions
    for site in df.site.unique():  # each field in turn
        train, test = df[df.site != site], df[df.site == site]  # split
        p = sm.calibrate_wcm(train.vv_db, train.sm, train[desc], train.incidence, groups=train.crop, bounds=bounds)  # fit on the others
        if test.crop.iloc[0] not in p.index:  # crop not calibrated
            continue
        pred = sm.invert_wcm(test.vv_db.values, test[desc].values, test.incidence.values, p.loc[test.crop.iloc[0]].to_dict())  # invert
        out.append(test.assign(pred=pred, mean_pred=train.sm.mean()))  # keep, with the campaign-mean baseline
    return pd.concat(out)  # all predictions


cv_free, cv = loo("ndvi"), loo("ndvi", BOUNDS)  # unconstrained and constrained
summary = pd.DataFrame({"WCM, unconstrained": sm.validation_metrics(cv_free.sm, cv_free.pred),

display(summary[["n", "bias", "rmse", "ubrmse", "pearson_r"]].astype(float).round(3))  # table

The honest test is sobering. The unconstrained model misses new fields by 0.20 m3 m-3 RMSE; the constrained model by 0.085, with a correlation of 0.22; and simply predicting the campaign mean, with no satellite at all, gives 0.039, because the soil moisture of these fields varied so little in this summer. The model only shows skill in August (R 0.35 over corn and 0.45 over soybean), when the range of conditions between fields was wider, and none in early June.

This is a common outcome of field campaigns, not a failure of the code. The ThetaProbe measures 0 to 5 cm and C-band senses 1 to 2 cm; soil sampling and the radar pass are up to a day apart; a 60 m window at the field centre represents a field only roughly; and roughness, row direction and residue differ between fields in ways the model does not know. Published WCM retrievals over crops reach 0.04 to 0.06 m3 m-3 mainly when parameters are calibrated per field over long time series, which removes the field-to-field differences that dominate here.

Bare-soil models in early June

On the first campaign dates most fields were bare or had small plants (NDVI below 0.4). There the canopy can be neglected and a surface scattering model applies directly.

Code 
bare = df[(df.ndvi < 0.4)].copy()  # nearly bare fields
print(f"{len(bare)} samples with NDVI < 0.4")  # sample size
grid_t = np.round(np.arange(0.10, 0.46, 0.01), 2)  # LUT soil moisture

for s_ in (0.7, 1.0, 1.5):  # Oh 2004 for three roughness values

display(pd.DataFrame({"observed VV": [bare.vv_db.mean()], **{f"Oh 2004, s = {s_} cm": [bare[f'oh_vv_s{s_}'].mean()] for s_ in (0.7, 1.0, 1.5)}}).round(2))  # mean VV
m = sm.validation_metrics(bare.sm, bare.iem_theta)  # IEM retrieval

Of the Oh 2004 curves, an rms height of 1.5 cm reproduces the mean early-June backscatter best (-7.7 against -8.2 dB observed), a typical value for tilled fields. The IEM look-up table inversion with roughness as a free parameter, however, does not retrieve the field differences (R -0.16, ubRMSE 0.10 m3 m-3): with a single polarization, a rougher dry field and a smoother wet field have the same VV, and the inversion cannot tell them apart. Multi-polarization or multi-angle data, or roughness measured in the field, are needed for bare-soil inversion.

Summary and exercises

  • SMAPVEX16 gives 135 matched samples of field soil moisture, Sentinel-1 backscatter on three orbits, incidence angle and Sentinel-2 NDVI over 32 corn and soybean fields.
  • The field soil moisture varied little (standard deviation 0.039 m3 m-3) and VV responded to it only in August; field-to-field roughness dominated in early June.
  • An unconstrained Water Cloud Model overfits (0.20 m3 m-3 RMSE at new fields); bounding the soil sensitivity to physical values brings it to 0.085, still above the 0.039 of predicting the mean.
  • The cross-polarization ratio is a usable vegetation descriptor without optical data.
  • Bare-soil inversion of a single polarization with unknown roughness is ill-posed.
  • Leave-one-field-out validation, and comparing against a no-skill baseline, are what reveal this; a calibration RMSE alone would have hidden it.
