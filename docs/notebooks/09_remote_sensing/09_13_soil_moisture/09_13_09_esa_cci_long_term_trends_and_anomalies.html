ESA CCI: Long-Term Trends and Anomalies

The ESA Climate Change Initiative Soil Moisture (CCI SM) record merges every suitable passive and active microwave sensor since 1978 into one daily dataset on a 0.25 degree grid. With more than 45 years it is long enough to ask climate questions: is the soil getting drier or wetter, and where? This notebook reads the COMBINED product over Bangladesh from 1979 to 2024, shows how the record changes as sensors come and go, builds monthly anomalies, and maps trends with the Mann-Kendall test and Sen’s slope for the whole year, the dry Rabi season and the monsoon.

Overview

Three products

Product Sensors Units
PASSIVE SMMR, SSM/I, TMI, AMSR-E, WindSat, AMSR2, SMOS, SMAP, GPM, FY-3 m3 m-3
ACTIVE ERS-1, ERS-2 AMI-WS, ASCAT on MetOp-A, B and C % saturation
COMBINED both, merged m3 m-3

How the merging works

Each sensor’s retrievals are rescaled to a common reference (GLDAS Noah land model soil moisture) by CDF matching, so they share one climatology; where several sensors overlap, their values are averaged with weights from triple collocation error estimates (Dorigo et al. 2017; Gruber et al. 2019), giving more weight to the sensor with the smaller error. Each value carries an uncertainty and flags (frozen soil, dense vegetation, no convergence, water).

Sensor eras and trends

The number and quality of sensors change over time: before 1987 only SMMR (every other day, coarse), from 2002 AMSR-E, from 2007 ASCAT, from 2010 SMOS, from 2015 SMAP. Coverage and noise change with them. Trends computed across sensor changes can be artefacts, so the analysis below checks the data density through time and uses robust, rank-based statistics.

Mann-Kendall and Sen’s slope

The Mann-Kendall test asks whether later values tend to be larger (or smaller) than earlier ones, using only the signs of all pairwise differences; it makes no assumption about the distribution. Sen’s slope is the median of all pairwise slopes, robust to outliers. Both are applied per cell to annual (or seasonal) means.

Long Records with GeoLattice

Function Purpose
sd.cci("COMBINED", sd.BANGLADESH, ...) (wraps sm.open_product("ESA_CCI", ...)) daily cube from the CEDA THREDDS server
sm.mask_quality CCI flags
sm.sm_climatology, sm.sm_anomaly climatology and anomalies
sm.trend Mann-Kendall test and Sen’s slope per cell

Learning objectives

  1. Read 46 years of merged satellite soil moisture for a country.
  2. Recognize sensor eras in the data density and uncertainty.
  3. Compute monthly anomalies and a national anomaly index.
  4. Map per-cell trends and their significance, and compare seasons.

Around 1980 a cell has a valid value on only about 35 days a year (SMMR observed every other day and many retrievals are flagged); the density doubles in the SSM/I era and reaches about 140 days a year after 2010. Even with many sensors, a large part of the year remains empty over Bangladesh because of dense vegetation, open water and flags in the monsoon. The median uncertainty falls from about 0.032 m3 m-3 in 1990 to 0.014 in 2015 to 2020. Annual means of the early years therefore rest on far fewer, noisier samples.

The national mean falls from about 0.24 m3 m-3 in January to its minimum of about 0.19 in March and April, at the end of the dry Rabi season (wheat, mustard and irrigated Boro rice), and peaks at about 0.36 in July with the monsoon. The February map shows the drier north-west (Rajshahi and Rangpur); in August the western and southern plains are the wettest. Monsoon values are the least reliable, since open water and flooded paddies fill many cells.

Trends per cell

Code 
recent = good.sel(time=slice("1991", "2024"))  # the period with SSM/I onwards
tr = sm.trend(recent, freq="YS", min_count=60)  # annual means with at least 60 days, Mann-Kendall and Sen
fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(13, 4.5))  # slope and significance
(tr.slope * 10).where(land).plot(ax=ax[0], cmap="BrBG", vmin=-0.03, vmax=0.03, cbar_kwargs={"label": "m3/m3 per decade"})  # Sen slope
yy, xx = np.meshgrid(tr.lat, tr.lon, indexing="ij"); sig = (tr.significant & land).values.astype(bool)  # significant cells
ax[0].scatter(xx[sig], yy[sig], s=4, c="k"); ax[0].set_title("Sen's slope 1991 to 2024 (dots: p < 0.05)")  # stippling
tr.p_value.where(land).plot(ax=ax[1], cmap="viridis_r", vmin=0, vmax=0.2); ax[1].set_title("Mann-Kendall p value")  # p values
for a in ax: bgd.boundary.plot(ax=a, color="k", lw=0.6); a.set_xlabel(""); a.set_ylabel("")  # outline
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()  # draw
s = (tr.slope * 10).where(land)  # per decade
print(f"cells drying: {float((s < 0).sum() / land.sum()):.0%}, significant drying {float(((s < 0) & tr.significant).sum() / land.sum()):.0%}; "
      f"significant wetting {float(((s > 0) & tr.significant).sum() / land.sum()):.0%}; median {float(s.median()):+.4f} m3/m3 per decade")  # summary
cells drying: 71%, significant drying 23%; significant wetting 0%; median -0.0029 m3/m3 per decade

Dry season and monsoon trends

Code 
rows = {}  # national trend per season
for name, months in (("Rabi (Dec to Mar)", [12, 1, 2, 3]), ("pre-monsoon (Apr to May)", [4, 5]), ("monsoon (Jun to Sep)", [6, 7, 8, 9])):  # seasons
    sub = recent.where(recent.time.dt.month.isin(months))  # one season
    shifted = sub.assign_coords(time=sub.time.to_index() + pd.DateOffset(months=1)) if 12 in months else sub  # December counts with the next year
    ts = shifted.mean(("lat", "lon")).resample(time="YS").mean().to_series().dropna()  # national seasonal mean per year
    t_ = sm.trend(xr.DataArray(ts.values, coords={"time": ts.index}, dims="time"), freq="YS")  # trend of the national series
    rows[name] = {"years": len(ts), "Sen slope (m3/m3 per decade)": float(t_.slope) * 10, "p": float(t_.p_value)}  # result
display(pd.DataFrame(rows).T.round(4))  # table
years Sen slope (m3/m3 per decade) p
Rabi (Dec to Mar) 35.0 0.0033 0.0381
pre-monsoon (Apr to May) 34.0 -0.0063 0.0353
monsoon (Jun to Sep) 34.0 -0.0045 0.0128

Since 1991, 71 % of the cells have a negative Sen’s slope and 23 % a significant drying trend (p < 0.05), concentrated in the west and north-west; no cell has a significant wetting trend. The median slope is small, about -0.003 m3 m-3 per decade. By season the national series dries in the pre-monsoon (-0.006 per decade) and monsoon (-0.005), while the dry Rabi season gets slightly wetter (+0.003), all significant at 5 %. A wetter dry season while the rest of the year dries is what the expansion of groundwater-irrigated Boro rice would produce. These trends are small against the year-to-year variability and comparable with the steps that sensor changes can introduce, so a significant trend in a merged record is a hypothesis to check against independent data (rain gauges, reanalysis, groundwater), not a finding on its own.

Summary and exercises

  • ESA CCI COMBINED gives 46 years of daily soil moisture over Bangladesh; data density rises from about 35 to 140 valid days per cell and year, and uncertainty halves, as sensors are added.
  • The national mean is lowest in March and April (about 0.19 m3 m-3) and highest in July (about 0.36).
  • The national anomaly index ranks the pre-monsoon of 2023 among the driest months on record.
  • Since 1991 a quarter of the cells dry significantly, mainly in the north-west, while the Rabi season gets slightly wetter; such trends must be weighed against sensor changes in the record.
