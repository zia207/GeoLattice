Soil Heat Flux, Roughness and Aerodynamic Resistance

After net radiation, two more pieces are needed before a model can solve for evapotranspiration. The soil heat flux G takes part of the net radiation into the ground, so it is not available for evaporation. The sensible heat flux H = rho cp dT / rah depends on how easily heat is carried from the surface into the air, which is expressed as an aerodynamic resistance rah. That resistance depends on wind, on the roughness of the surface and on how the air’s own buoyancy helps or hinders turbulence (atmospheric stability). This notebook estimates G with the SEBAL and METRIC formulas, builds roughness maps from LAI, NDVI and crop type, follows the wind from the 200 m blending height to the surface, and shows with a small experiment why stability corrections can change H by a factor of two to three.

Overview

Soil heat flux

G is largest over bare, dark, hot soil in full sun and small under a dense canopy, which shades the ground. Satellite models estimate the ratio G/Rn from vegetation and temperature:

Model Formula
SEBAL (Bastiaanssen 2000) G/Rn = (Ts - 273.15) (0.0038 albedo + 0.0074 albedo^2) (1 - 0.98 NDVI^4) / albedo
METRIC (Tasumi 2003), LAI of at least 0.5 G/Rn = 0.05 + 0.18 exp(-0.521 LAI)
METRIC, LAI below 0.5 G/Rn = 1.80 (Ts - 273.15) / Rn + 0.084
Water G/Rn about 0.5 for clear water in midday (heat stored in the water column)

The ratios apply around midday; over a whole day G is close to zero, which is why daily ET uses Rn - G only at the instant of the image.

Wind near the surface

Under neutral conditions wind increases with the logarithm of height:

u(z) = (u* / k) ln((z - d) / zom)

u* is the friction velocity (the shear the surface exerts), k = 0.41 the von Karman constant, zom the momentum roughness length (roughly 1/8 of the canopy height) and d the displacement height (about 2/3 of the canopy height). At a blending height of about 200 m the wind is assumed to be the same over every surface in the scene, so wind measured at one weather station is extended to 200 m with the station’s grass roughness and brought back down over each pixel with that pixel’s zom.

Aerodynamic resistance and the METRIC trick

Heat is carried from the surface by turbulence with a resistance

rah = (ln(z2 / z1) - psi_h(z2) + psi_h(z1)) / (u* k)

SEBAL and METRIC evaluate it between two heights above the zero plane displacement, z1 = 0.1 m and z2 = 2 m, and define dT as the temperature difference between those heights, not between the surface and the air. This sidesteps two unknowns: the roughness length for heat (zoh, much smaller than zom and poorly known) and the difference between radiometric and aerodynamic temperature. The calibration of dT at the anchors (notebook 05) absorbs them.

Stability

When the surface is much hotter than the air, rising warm air enhances turbulence (unstable conditions, typical of dry land at midday): rah falls and H rises. When the surface is cooler than the air (irrigated crops under hot air), turbulence is damped (stable): rah rises. The Monin-Obukhov length

L = -rho cp u*^3 Ts / (k g H)

measures the balance between shear and buoyancy. Negative L is unstable, positive stable, infinite neutral. The corrections psi_m and psi_h follow Paulson (1970) for unstable and Webb (1970) for stable air. Because L depends on H and H on rah, they are solved by iteration.

Soil Heat and Aerodynamics with GeoLattice

Function Purpose
seb.soil_heat_flux(rn, lst, albedo, ndvi, method=) G with the SEBAL ("bastiaanssen") or METRIC ("metric") formula
seb.momentum_roughness(lai=, ndvi=, height=, landcover=, table=) zom from LAI, NDVI, canopy height or a land cover table
seb.wind_at_blending_height, seb.wind_2m wind profiles
seb.friction_velocity, seb.aerodynamic_resistance u* and rah
seb.monin_obukhov_length, seb.stability_corrections stability

Learning objectives

  1. Compute G with the SEBAL and METRIC formulas and explain where they differ.
  2. Describe the log wind profile, roughness length, displacement height and blending height.
  3. Build roughness maps from LAI, NDVI and crop type, and see how they change rah.
  4. Explain why METRIC evaluates rah between 0.1 and 2 m.
  5. Iterate H, the Monin-Obukhov length and the stability corrections, and quantify their effect.

Both formulas give G/Rn of 0.3 over fallow land and rangeland, and METRIC falls to about 0.06 at the LAI cap of 6. METRIC’s bare-soil branch (LAI below 0.5) depends on surface temperature, which shows as the vertical spread at LAI 0 in the scatter. The two formulas agree over fallow land (144 and 146 W m-2) and disagree over vegetation, where SEBAL gives 27 to 41 W m-2 more (G/Rn of 0.20 to 0.23 against 0.13 to 0.18). Differences of that size in G pass straight into LE; the anchors calibrate them away only at the two anchor pixels. The chapter uses the METRIC formula.

The wind profile and the blending height

At the overpass ERA5 gives wind at 10 m. seb.wind_2m brings it to 2 m with the FAO-56 profile, and seb.wind_at_blending_height extends it to 200 m over a grass weather station.

Code 
u10 = ds.attrs["u10"]; u2 = p["u2"]; u200 = p["u200"]  # ERA5 wind at 10 m, at 2 m, and at 200 m
k = seb.VON_KARMAN  # 0.41
print(f"wind: {u10:.2f} m/s at 10 m, {u2:.2f} m/s at 2 m, {u200:.2f} m/s at 200 m")  # the profile
z = np.geomspace(0.05, 200, 200)  # heights, m
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 5))  # one panel
for zom, lab in [(0.005, "bare soil, zom 0.005 m"), (0.0144, "grass station, zom 0.0144 m"), (0.06, "row crop, zom 0.06 m"), (0.4, "orchard, zom 0.4 m")]:  # surfaces
    us = float(seb.friction_velocity(u200, 200.0, zom))  # neutral u* that gives the same wind at 200 m
    ax.plot(np.where(z > zom, us / k * np.log(z / zom), np.nan), z, label=f"{lab}, u* = {us:.2f} m/s")  # log profile
ax.set_yscale("log"); ax.set_xlabel("wind speed (m/s)"); ax.set_ylabel("height (m)")  # axes
ax.axhline(200, color="k", lw=0.6, ls="--"); ax.legend(fontsize=8); ax.set_title("Same wind at 200 m, different surfaces")  # labels

wind: 2.16 m/s at 10 m, 1.61 m/s at 2 m, 3.12 m/s at 200 m

All profiles meet at 200 m. A rough orchard slows the wind near the ground more, but its friction velocity, the turbulent mixing that carries heat, is 1.75 times that of bare soil (0.21 against 0.12 m/s). That is why rough canopies transfer heat efficiently even with modest wind.

Roughness length

Three common ways to map zom:

  • LAI (METRIC, agricultural areas): zom = 0.018 LAI, at least 0.005 m.
  • NDVI (SEBAL and geeSEBAL): zom = exp(5.62 NDVI - 5.809).
  • Land cover table: typical canopy heights per crop class and zom = 0.123 h. The heights below are illustrative values for mature crops in midsummer, not measurements.

          36: 0.5, 208: 0.3, 24: 0.1, 21: 0.1, 51: 0.4, 61: 0.05, 176: 0.2, 152: 0.5, 121: 1.0, 122: 2.0,

The three methods disagree most over orchards: LAI and NDVI see partial canopies (LAI near 1.2) and give 0.02 to 0.07 m, against 0.45 m from tree height, while tall trees in rows are aerodynamically rough. The METRIC manual adds a Perrier function for tall, sparse canopies for this reason. For annual crops and bare soil the methods are closer. The next section shows what these differences do to rah.

Friction velocity and aerodynamic resistance

Under neutral conditions, u* = k u200 / ln(200 / zom) and rah between 0.1 and 2 m is ln(20) / (k u*).

Code 
rah = {}  # neutral rah for each roughness map
for name, zz in [("LAI", zom_lai), ("NDVI", zom_ndvi), ("crop table", zom_tab)]:  # three roughness maps
    ustar = seb.friction_velocity(u200, 200.0, zz)  # m/s
    rah[name] = seb.aerodynamic_resistance(ustar, z1=0.1, z2=2.0)  # s/m
display(by_group(**{f"rah {n}": r for n, r in rah.items()}).round(0))  # per crop group, s/m
fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(13, 4.8))  # map and histogram

ax[0].set_title("Neutral rah (zom from LAI)"); ax[0].axis("off")  # title
for n, r in rah.items():  # every roughness map

With about 3 m/s at 200 m, neutral rah is roughly 35 to 60 s/m: about 60 s/m over smooth fallow land and down to 35 s/m over orchards with the crop-table roughness. For the same dT, a field with rah of 35 s/m has 1.7 times the sensible heat of one with 60 s/m, so roughness is a first-order input. In SEBAL and METRIC its error is partly absorbed by the calibration of dT, which is computed with the same rah at the anchors.

Stability: a numerical experiment

The experiment holds the wind (3.1 m/s at 200 m) and roughness fixed and asks: for a given temperature difference dT between 0.1 and 2 m, what is H under neutral assumptions and after the Monin-Obukhov iteration? It repeats the inner loop of seb.sensible_heat_iterative without the anchor calibration. dT stays below 10 K: at the hot anchor of this scene METRIC converges at about 5 K, because H cannot exceed the available energy Rn - G.

Code 
def h_iterated(dT, zom, u_blend=u200, t_surface=320.0, pressure=100.5, n_iter=30):  # H for a fixed dT, with stability
    psi_m200 = psi_h2 = psi_h01 = 0.0  # start neutral
    rho = float(seb.air_density(pressure, t_surface - dT))  # air density, kg/m3
    for _ in range(n_iter):  # iterate H, L and the corrections
        ustar = seb.friction_velocity(u_blend, 200.0, zom, psi_m200)  # m/s
        rah_ = seb.aerodynamic_resistance(ustar, z1=0.1, z2=2.0, psi_h_z2=psi_h2, psi_h_z1=psi_h01)  # s/m
        h = rho * seb.CP * dT / rah_  # W/m2
        L = seb.monin_obukhov_length(rho, ustar, t_surface, h)  # m
        pm200, _ = seb.stability_corrections(200.0, L)  # momentum at 200 m (unstable)
        pm2, psi_h2 = seb.stability_corrections(2.0, L)  # heat at 2 m (and momentum at 2 m for stable air)
        _, psi_h01 = seb.stability_corrections(0.1, L)  # heat at 0.1 m
        psi_m200 = np.where(L < 0, pm200, pm2)  # METRIC uses 2 m for the stable momentum term
    return float(h), float(rah_), float(L)  # final values

Below 1 K the corrections are modest. At the dT of a hot anchor (about 5 K), unstable air lowers rah and raises H to about three times the neutral estimate over smooth surfaces, less over rough ones, which are already well mixed. This matches what SEBAL and METRIC do at their hot pixels: in the notebook 00 run of this scene, rah at the hot anchor fell from 60 s/m (neutral) to 17 s/m. With negative dT (a surface cooler than the air) stable conditions damp H and raise rah. Leaving stability out would bias the hot anchor, and through the calibration every pixel. Notebook 05 runs the same loop inside SEBAL, where dT itself is recalibrated at every iteration.

Summary and exercises

  • G/Rn is about 0.3 over hot bare soil and below 0.1 under dense crops; the SEBAL and METRIC formulas differ most over partial canopies.
  • Wind measured at a station is carried to a 200 m blending height and back down over each pixel’s roughness; an orchard has about 1.75 times the friction velocity of bare soil.
  • LAI, NDVI and crop-table roughness disagree most over orchards, which LAI and NDVI see as partial canopies.
  • METRIC’s rah between 0.1 and 2 m avoids the roughness length for heat and the radiometric versus aerodynamic temperature problem; the anchor calibration absorbs them.
  • Neutral rah between 0.1 and 2 m was 35 to 60 s/m. Stability corrections raise H about threefold at a hot anchor’s dT of 5 K over smooth surfaces and must be iterated.
