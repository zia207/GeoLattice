Sentinel-5P and Sentinel-5: Mapping Air Pollution from Orbit

Sentinel-5P (Copernicus, ESA) carries TROPOMI, a spectrometer that retrieves trace gas columns such as NO2, SO2, CO, CH4 and HCHO, plus aerosol and cloud information, from daily global coverage.

Overview

Its successor, Sentinel-5, began flying in 2025 on EUMETSAT’s MetOp-SG A satellites. This module works with real Sentinel-5P data over two whole AOIs: Bangladesh on an ordinary dry season day, and New York State during a Canadian wildfire smoke event. Products are read, masked and gridded with geolattice.rsense.atmosphere.sentinel_5p, clipped to the administrative boundary, and resampled to a 1 km grid with a thin plate spline.

Core concepts

Learning objectives

  1. Know the Sentinel-5P product family and the qa_value quality convention.
  2. Read a real TROPOMI Level 2 product from its nested PRODUCT group.
  3. Select the regional segment(s) that cover an entire country or state from a Planetary Computer STAC search, including the case where two adjacent segments are both needed.
  4. Clip quality-filtered swath points to an exact administrative boundary polygon.
  5. Resample irregular swath points onto a regular 1 km grid with a thin plate spline, in a projected (metres) coordinate system, and understand why that differs from simple binning.
  6. Build a real six-pollutant picture of a whole country (Bangladesh, dry season) and of a single transient smoke event over a US state (Canadian wildfire smoke over New York).
  7. Recognize a genuine data availability limit (methane under heavy smoke) rather than mistake it for a bug.
  8. Check processing consistency across the files a single download actually needs to combine.

The two missions

“Sentinel-5” covers two related missions for monitoring atmospheric composition.

  • Sentinel-5 Precursor (Sentinel-5P): a dedicated satellite carrying the TROPOMI instrument, in orbit since 2017.
  • Sentinel-5: a spectrometer carried on EUMETSAT’s MetOp Second Generation (MetOp-SG) A-type weather satellites. The first unit, Sentinel-5A, launched in August 2025.

Heritage. These missions build on earlier European and Dutch atmospheric spectrometers: GOME on ERS-2 (1995), SCIAMACHY on Envisat (2002 to 2012), OMI on NASA’s Aura satellite (2004, a Dutch-Finnish instrument), and GOME-2 on the first-generation MetOp satellites.

Why a precursor? Sentinel-5P was designed to close the data gap between the loss of Envisat and the dedicated Copernicus atmospheric missions, Sentinel-4 and Sentinel-5.

Sentinel-5P - Launched on 13 October 2017 from the Plesetsk Cosmodrome in Russia on a Rockot. - TROPOMI was co-funded by the Netherlands and ESA. KNMI handles mission planning for the instrument, and ESOC handles mission control. - Design life was about 7 years. Operations are secured until the end of 2027. - Its loose formation with Suomi-NPP ends by Q4 2026. Suomi-NPP data delivery itself stops on 1 November 2026, so the cloud products that rely on VIIRS will be affected.

Sentinel-5 (on MetOp-SG) - The first MetOp-SG satellite, MetOp-SG-A1, launched on 13 August 2025 on an Ariane 6 from Kourou, carrying the Sentinel-5A instrument. - Airbus Defence and Space is the prime contractor for the Sentinel-5 instrument. - Sentinel-5A has returned its first gas maps and is in commissioning and calibration and validation. Full operational products will follow once that phase ends. - Sentinel-5B is planned for launch in 2033. A third unit (5C) will fly on MetOp-SG-A3, extending the series into the 2040s.

Sentinel-4 (related mission). A geostationary instrument on Meteosat Third Generation (MTG-S1), launched on 1 July 2025. It gives hourly coverage of Europe and complements the daily global view from Sentinel-5 and Sentinel-5P.

Why the two missions complement each other - The morning (Sentinel-5) and afternoon (Sentinel-5P) overpasses together capture the daily cycle of pollutants such as NO2. - MetOp-SG-A1 also carries other instruments whose data can be combined with Sentinel-5: IASI-NG (an infrared sounder), METimage (an imager), and 3MI (a multi-viewing polarimeter for aerosols).

Sensors

TROPOMI (TROPOspheric Monitoring Instrument), on Sentinel-5P - Nadir-viewing push-broom grating imaging spectrometer with a 108 degree field of view. - Four spectrometers with 2D detectors and about 4,000 spectral channels. - Swath: about 2,600 km. - Spatial sampling: 5.5 by 3.5 km at nadir since 6 August 2019 (originally 7 by 3.5 km); SWIR bands 5.5 by 7 km; band 1 (deep UV) coarser. - Spectral range: UV-visible (270 to 495 nm), NIR (675 to 775 nm), and SWIR (2305 to 2385 nm).

Sentinel-5 UVNS (Ultraviolet-Visible-Near-infrared-Shortwave infrared) spectrometer, on MetOp-SG A - Push-broom grating spectrometer with five spectrometers, a direct evolution of TROPOMI. - Swath: about 2,670 km. - Spatial sampling: about 7.5 by 7 km at nadir; UV1 is coarser, about 50 by 50 km. - New SWIR-1 band at about 1.6 micrometres. Combined with the 2.3 micrometre band, it improves methane and CO retrievals and helps characterize aerosol and cloud effects.

Product format - NetCDF-4, organized in groups: PRODUCT (main variables plus qa_value) and SUPPORT_DATA (geolocation, pixel corners, input data, detailed results). - Units are mol per square metre. Each variable includes conversion attributes, for example multiplication_factor_to_convert_to_molecules_percm2. - Rough conversions: mol/m2 times 6.022e19 gives molecules/cm2. For ozone, mol/m2 times 2241.15 gives Dobson Units.

Recommended quality thresholds (qa_value): above 0.75 for tropospheric NO2; above 0.5 for most other products (CO, CH4, HCHO, SO2, O3).

Level 3. Gridded L3 products are available in Google Earth Engine and through NASA tools. You can also grid L2 data yourself, as this notebook does.

Sentinel-5 (MetOp-SG) - Level 1B: Earth radiance, solar irradiance, and calibration products, which feed Level-2 processing. - Level 2 (EUMETSAT, planned): O3 (total, tropospheric, profile); NO2, SO2, HCHO, CHOCHO (glyoxal), BrO; CO, CH4; aerosol (absorbing aerosol index, layer height, optical depth); cloud and surface UV. - Products will be distributed by EUMETSAT after commissioning. Formats are expected to stay close to the Sentinel-5P conventions.

Applications

  • Air quality. Mapping NO2 and SO2 from traffic, power plants, shipping, and industry; estimating emissions with top-down inversions; inputs to air-quality forecasts from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS). TROPOMI famously recorded the sharp NO2 drops during the 2020 COVID-19 lockdowns.
  • Methane. Detecting super-emitters from oil and gas, coal mines, and landfills. Methane data feed UNEP IMEO’s Methane Alert and Response System (MARS) and national inventory checks.
  • Ozone and UV. Ozone hole monitoring, stratospheric ozone trends, and UV index forecasts.
  • Volcanic hazards. SO2 and ash plume tracking for aviation safety through the Volcanic Ash Advisory Centres.
  • Wildfires and biomass burning. CO and aerosol plume transport, smoke-height estimates.
  • Climate. Greenhouse gas monitoring (CH4) and aerosol-cloud interactions; long-term records continued from GOME, SCIAMACHY, and OMI.
  • Vegetation. Solar-induced chlorophyll fluorescence from TROPOMI’s NIR bands, used as a research product for photosynthesis.
  • Health and epidemiology. Exposure estimates of NO2 and HCHO for linking air pollution to health outcomes; environmental justice analyses at neighbourhood scale; county-level panel datasets.

Python and software tools: xarray and netCDF4 for reading (open the group with group="PRODUCT"); HARP (harpconvert) for filtering by quality and gridding L2 swaths into L3; satpy (tropomi_l2 reader) and pyresample; earthengine-api and geemap for the L3 collections; openeo and pystac-client for access through CDSE; ESA’s Atmospheric Toolbox (HARP, CODA, VISAN). geolattice.rsense.atmosphere.sentinel_5p, used throughout this notebook, covers the reading, masking, gridding, and emission-estimation steps directly in Python.

Practical tips: always filter by qa_value before averaging; use OFFL or RPRO products for trend analysis; average over at least monthly windows, since daily NO2 is noisy and cloud-affected; use averaging kernels when comparing with models or ground stations; when merging Sentinel-5P (13:30) with Sentinel-5 (09:30), account for the overpass-time difference.

Boundaries, regional segments and the clip-then-resample workflow

A STAC search over a whole country’s bounding box returns every regional segment that intersects it. For a small city-sized area, one segment (about five minutes of orbit) is always enough; for a larger area such as the whole of Bangladesh or New York State, the satellite can need two adjacent segments to cover the full north-south extent in a single overpass, as the Case study B: New York State under Canadian wildfire smoke, 15 July 2026 section finds. The filter that keeps a single-segment download small, excluding the whole orbit duplicate by its scanline count (s5p:shape[0] < 1000), still applies here and is just as necessary: skip it and every download balloons to the full ~600 MB orbit file. The one exception is methane, which, depending on the exact day and AOI, sometimes has no small regional segment at all and needs the whole orbit granule regardless – download_segments below falls back to it automatically when that happens.

Three steps turn a downloaded segment into a clean 1 km map:

  1. Clip. Keep only swath pixel centres that fall inside the exact administrative boundary polygon (shapely.contains_xy), not just the bounding box – a country or state is never a rectangle. Two adjacent segments can repeat one scan line at their shared boundary; average duplicate geolocations before fitting anything, rather than letting the same site enter the interpolator twice.
  2. Project. Reproject the clipped points into a local projected CRS (metres), since a thin plate spline measures distance between points and a degree of longitude is not a fixed distance except exactly at the equator.
  3. Resample. Fit scipy.interpolate.RBFInterpolator with kernel="thin_plate_spline" on the projected points, and evaluate it on a regular 1 km grid built from the boundary’s own projected bounds. neighbors=25 makes this a local thin plate spline, using only each target cell’s nearest control points rather than solving one global system – the only way this stays fast with tens of thousands of 1 km target cells. Pixels outside the exact boundary are masked back out to NaN after interpolation, so the TPS surface fitted from points near the border never leaks into a neighbouring country or state in the final map.

# download every segment of a product that covers the box on a date
def download_segments(gas, bbox, date, out_dir):
    """Download every regional segment of `gas` that intersects `bbox` on `date` (falling back
    to the whole orbit granule when no regional segment exists), skipping files already on
    disk. Returns the list of local paths."""
    # One calendar day. limit=1000 is high enough that a country-sized box is not truncated.
    search = CATALOG.search(collections=["sentinel-5p-l2-netcdf"], bbox=bbox,
                            datetime=f"{date}T00:00:00Z/{date}T23:59:59Z", limit=1000)
    # A bbox search returns every gas. Keep only the collection prefix for this one.
    items = [it for it in search.items() if it.id.startswith(GAS_COLLECTION_PREFIX[gas])]
    # Prefer the short regional segments. shape[0] is the along-track scanline count.
    small = [it for it in items if it.properties.get("s5p:shape", [9999])[0] < MAX_SCANLINES]
    # Methane sometimes has no regional segment; fall back to the full-orbit granule.
    chosen = small if small else items
    dest_dir = out_dir / gas  # folder for this product
    dest_dir.mkdir(exist_ok=True, parents=True)  # create it
    paths = []  # paths of the downloaded files
    for it in chosen:  # every chosen segment
        dest = dest_dir / f"{it.id}.nc"  # local file
        # Skip the HTTP request when a previous run already saved this granule.
        if not dest.exists():
            # Stream in 1 MB chunks so a large orbit file is not held entirely in memory.
            with requests.get(it.assets[GAS_ASSET_KEY[gas]].href, timeout=300, stream=True) as r:
                r.raise_for_status()  # stop on an HTTP error
                with open(dest, "wb") as f:  # write the file ...
                    for chunk in r.iter_content(chunk_size=1 << 20):  # ... in 1 MB chunks
                        f.write(chunk)  # append the chunk
        paths.append(dest)  # remember the path
    return paths  # the downloaded files

Reading, clipping and resampling

read_clip_points keeps the quality-filtered pixel centres that fall inside the administrative boundary and averages any duplicate location where two segments overlap. tps_resample fits a local thin plate spline in the projected coordinate system and evaluates it on a 1 km grid. process_gas runs that sequence for one product, and plot_grid draws the result with robust colour limits so a few overshoot pixels do not stretch the scale.

Code 
# Helpers that turn downloaded segments into a boundary-clipped 1 km map.

def read_clip_points(reader, var, paths, boundary_geo, convert=None, min_qa=None):
    # quality-filtered swath points inside a boundary
    """Read one or more Sentinel-5P segments, mask by qa_value, optionally convert units,
    concatenate, and keep only the pixel centres that fall inside `boundary_geo` (EPSG:4326).
    Adjacent segments can share one scan line at their boundary -- duplicate geolocations are
    averaged rather than left as two separate control points at the same site."""
    lons, lats, vals = [], [], []  # longitude, latitude and value of all files
    for p in paths:  # every file
        ds = reader(p)  # read it
        # qa_mask reads the recommended cutoff from the file unless min_qa overrides it.
        valid = s5p.qa_mask(ds["qa_value"], min_qa=min_qa)
        clean = ds[var].where(valid)  # values that pass the quality test
        if convert is not None:  # a unit conversion is given
            clean = convert(clean)  # apply it
        # Ravel keeps one row per swath pixel so later steps can treat the scene as points.
        lons.append(ds.longitude.values.ravel())
        lats.append(ds.latitude.values.ravel())  # latitudes
        vals.append(clean.values.ravel())  # values
    lon, lat, val = np.concatenate(lons), np.concatenate(lats), np.concatenate(vals)  # all files together
    # Drop pixels the quality mask or the retrieval itself set to NaN.
    ok = np.isfinite(val)
    lon, lat, val = lon[ok], lat[ok], val[ok]  # keep finite values
    # contains_xy tests the exact polygon, so pixels in the bounding box but outside the border go.
    inside = shapely.contains_xy(boundary_geo, lon, lat)
    lon, lat, val = lon[inside], lat[inside], val[inside]  # keep points inside the boundary
    # Two adjacent segments can repeat one scan line. Average those duplicates before fitting.
    dedup = (pd.DataFrame({"lon": lon, "lat": lat, "val": val})
            .groupby(["lon", "lat"], as_index=False)["val"].mean())
    return dedup["lon"].to_numpy(), dedup["lat"].to_numpy(), dedup["val"].to_numpy()  # one value per location


# resample scattered points onto a regular grid with a thin plate spline
def tps_resample(lon, lat, val, boundary_proj, dst_crs, res_m=1000.0, neighbors=25):
    """Thin plate spline resample of clipped, quality-filtered swath points onto a regular
    `res_m` grid covering `boundary_proj`'s own bounds (already in `dst_crs`, metres), masked
    back to the exact boundary. Returns (x centres, y centres, 2D grid)."""
    # Project the control points into metres. A degree of longitude is not a fixed distance.
    transformer = pyproj.Transformer.from_crs("EPSG:4326", dst_crs, always_xy=True)
    x, y = transformer.transform(lon, lat)  # points in the projected CRS
    minx, miny, maxx, maxy = boundary_proj.bounds  # bounds of the boundary
    xs = np.arange(minx, maxx, res_m)  # x coordinates of the grid
    ys = np.arange(maxy, miny, -res_m)   # descending: row 0 is the northern edge
    gx, gy = np.meshgrid(xs, ys)  # grid of x and y
    # neighbors=25 keeps the spline local: each cell uses only its nearest control points.
    interp = RBFInterpolator(np.column_stack([x, y]), val, kernel="thin_plate_spline", neighbors=neighbors)
    # interpolated values at every grid cell
    pred = interp(np.column_stack([gx.ravel(), gy.ravel()])).reshape(gx.shape)
    # geometry_mask with invert=True is True inside the polygon. Everything else becomes NaN,
    # so the fitted surface does not leak across the border.
    transform = Affine(res_m, 0, minx, 0, -res_m, maxy)
    # cells inside the boundary
    mask = rasterio.features.geometry_mask([boundary_proj], out_shape=gx.shape, transform=transform, invert=True)
    pred = np.where(mask, pred, np.nan)  # NaN outside it
    # Shift from cell edges to cell centres so the map extent lines up with the grid.
    return xs + res_m / 2, ys - res_m / 2, pred


def robust_vlim(grid, low=1, high=99, floor_zero=False):  # colour limits from percentiles
    """2nd-98th-percentile colour limits -- keeps a handful of TPS overshoot pixels near sparse
    edges from stretching the whole colour scale. `floor_zero` additionally clips the lower
    limit at 0 for quantities (columns, mixing ratios) that cannot be physically negative."""
    vmin, vmax = np.nanpercentile(grid, [low, high])  # low and high percentiles
    return (max(vmin, 0.0) if floor_zero else vmin), vmax  # optionally start at zero


MIN_TPS_POINTS = 20   # below this, a 1 km surface is not a resampling, it is an extrapolation

def process_gas(gas, paths, aoi, min_qa=None):  # read, quality-filter, clip and resample one product
    """Full pipeline for one product: read, qa-mask, clip, dedupe, then TPS-resample to 1 km.
    Returns (lon, lat, val, gx, gy, grid); grid is None when too few points survive clipping."""
    spec = PRODUCTS[gas]  # settings of this product
    lon, lat, val = read_clip_points(spec["reader"], spec["var"], paths, aoi["geo"],  # the clipped points
                                     convert=spec["convert"], min_qa=min_qa)
    # Too few points would let the spline invent a smooth surface across the whole area.
    if lon.size < MIN_TPS_POINTS:
        return lon, lat, val, None, None, None  # too few points: no grid
    gx, gy, grid = tps_resample(lon, lat, val, aoi["proj"], aoi["crs"])  # resample to 1 km
    return lon, lat, val, gx, gy, grid  # points and grid


def plot_grid(ax, gx, gy, grid, spec, title):  # draw one gridded product
    """Draw one resampled grid with robust colour limits and a colourbar in the product's units."""
    vmin, vmax = robust_vlim(grid, floor_zero=spec["floor_zero"])  # robust colour limits
    # extent is the projected cell-centre range; origin="upper" matches the north-to-south rows.
    im = ax.imshow(grid, cmap=spec["cmap"], extent=[gx.min(), gx.max(), gy.min(), gy.max()],
                   origin="upper", vmin=vmin, vmax=vmax)
    ax.set_title(title)  # title
    ax.set_xticks([]); ax.set_yticks([])  # no ticks
    plt.colorbar(im, ax=ax, shrink=0.75, label=spec["unit"])  # colour bar with units
    return im  # the image

Summary, exercises and references

Key ideas

  • Sentinel-5P’s TROPOMI measures NO2, SO2, CO, CH4, HCHO, ozone, aerosol and cloud from a single instrument, daily, at 5.5 by 3.5 km, with a swath wide enough to cover a whole country or state in one or two regional segments rather than a tile mosaic.
  • Always filter by qa_value. The right threshold differs by product and is written into the variable’s own metadata, not fixed across the mission.
  • Clip to the exact administrative boundary polygon, not just its bounding box, and average any duplicate geolocations where adjacent regional segments overlap, before fitting anything.
  • A thin plate spline, fitted in a projected (metres) CRS and evaluated on a regular 1 km grid, turns a sparse, irregular swath into a usable dense raster where simple cell averaging would leave most cells empty – at the cost of some overshoot near sparse edges, handled here with robust percentile colour limits rather than the raw data range.
  • Bangladesh’s six products, mapped on an ordinary dry season day, each tell a related but distinct story: NO2 and SO2 mark combustion point sources, CO carries a longer-lived regional background, CH4 reflects the delta’s agriculture more than any city, and aerosol index gives a clean single number for the dry season haze.
  • The same pipeline applied to New York under Canadian wildfire smoke shows CO, HCHO and especially aerosol index responding strongly to a transient plume that NO2 and SO2, both tied to local anthropogenic sources, do not track.
  • A genuine data availability limit, methane’s retrieval collapsing under heavy smoke, is a real scientific finding worth reporting plainly (the Methane: a second data availability case study section), not a bug to paper over with a misleading extrapolated map.
  • Check processing consistency across any files a single analysis combines, including two regional segments of the same overpass, not only a multi-date time series.
